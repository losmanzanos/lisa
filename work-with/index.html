Ring 02 · Who This Is For

Four kinds of people keep finding me.

Not a complete list. Just the honest one. Individuals only, no couples.

What they have in common

Roughly fifty one percent of the way in.

It isn't a diagnosis. It's that something has already tipped. My clients are usually past the question of whether to do this, even if they're nowhere near sure what they want out of it.

Most have been to therapy before. Often it was fine and it taught them some skills, and they're back because they want something with more depth than skills this time.

If you're still genuinely undecided about being in therapy at all, that's allowed. I'd just rather you spend a free twenty minute call asking me about it than book eight sessions to find out.

The four

Each has a page of its own.

  • Ages 16 to 24

    Young adults

    The questions turn enormous and the language for them arrives late. I talk to young adults as people capable of depth, because they are.

  • Already introspective

    People who already look inward

    You journal, you notice, you have the vocabulary. What's missing is rhythm, and a second read on what you keep circling.

  • Queer and trans clients

    LGBTQIA+

    WPATH trained, queer myself, three years at an LGBTQIA+ practice. You can skip the part where you explain yourself first.

  • Faith and its aftermath

    Religious trauma

    Deconstruction, reconstruction, and the long middle where you're not sure which one you're doing.

People who already look inward

There's no separate page for this one. It's here.

Some people arrive with the groundwork laid. You've done the reading. You keep a journal. You can name a feeling while it's still happening, which is genuinely rarer than it sounds.

The work is different then. Less teaching, more interpretation. I'm not going to walk you through identifying an emotion. What I can do is help with timing, with precision, and with the question underneath all that noticing, which is usually some version of so what do I do now.

You have the material. What you want is a way to read it.

Three things tend to come up: when to stay with something and when to set it down, finding words exact enough to hold what you actually mean, and understanding what the noticing has been pointing at the whole time.

Who this isn't for

Said plainly, so nobody wastes a consult.

I don't work with couples.

I don't prescribe, and I'm not the right fit if medication management is the most urgent thing on your list, or if you need a higher level of care than weekly outpatient therapy. I'll coordinate with a prescriber happily. I just can't be one.

My clients start at sixteen. If you're younger than that I'm not your therapist, though I can usually point you toward someone who is.
