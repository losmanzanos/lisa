People ask about the name, usually with a slightly raised eyebrow, which is fair. It sounds like a claim.

It comes from Naropa, where the contemplative psychotherapy program uses the phrase brilliant sanity to describe what's underneath everything a person brings into a room. Not their coping. Not their diagnosis. The thing that was there first and stays there regardless.

I didn't believe it when I first heard it. I'd come through a long stretch of pain and depression, and nobody looking at me in those years would have reached for the word sane. I was functional on my better days and barely that on the rest.

> The premise isn't that you're fine. It's that what you're doing made sense.

What changed my mind was sitting still for long enough to watch my own mind do its thing. Not fixing it. Watching it. And noticing that underneath the noise there was something that hadn't been damaged, that was just waiting around for me to stop drowning it out.

That's the whole premise. Societal expectation, trauma, how you were raised, the specific circumstances you landed in: those weren't in your control, and your responses to them were reasonable. What's in your control is how you relate to yourself now.

The joke in the initials is intentional, by the way. Therapy that takes itself too seriously tends to miss things.
