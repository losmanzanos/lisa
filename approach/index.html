Contemplative psychotherapy

Naropa University, 2017.

Most models open by naming what's broken. This one opens somewhere else: underneath whatever brought you here, you're already basically sane. Naropa calls it brilliant sanity. I named the practice after it because it's the one idea I'd keep if I had to throw out everything else.

That isn't positive thinking. Plenty is genuinely hard and some of it was done to you. The premise is just that the hard parts are responses rather than defects.

Social expectation, trauma, how you were raised, what your circumstances have been: none of that was in your control. How you relate to yourself is, and that's where the room to move is.

Internal Family Systems

Level I trained.

IFS assumes you aren't one voice but several, and that even the harshest one is trying to protect you. The part that keeps you awake rehearsing conversations isn't your enemy. It has a job, and it's been doing that job since long before you could have chosen otherwise.

So we get curious about those parts instead of arguing with them. People tend to soften toward themselves faster this way than they do by being talked out of anything.

EMDR

For memory that still has teeth.

Some memories never finished processing. They arrive at full volume, out of order, with your body behaving as though it's happening now rather than then.

EMDR works on that directly. I don't use it with everyone and I rarely use it early. When it fits, it can shift something that years of talking has only circled.

Mindfulness

Certified instructor, Naropa and iBme.

I teach mindfulness and I practice it. Those are different things and both matter.

In session it's seldom a formal exercise. More often it's me asking what's happening in your body right now, then being quiet while you find out. That pause is where a surprising amount of the useful material shows up.

I also run mindfulness groups for teens. Details on those are still coming together.

Gender affirming care

WPATH trained.

I'm WPATH trained and I spent three years at an LGBTQIA+ practice before going out on my own. If you need letters or coordination with other providers, ask on the consult call and I'll tell you plainly what I can and can't do.
