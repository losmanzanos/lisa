Who I work with

Faith, taken apart.

Deconstruction, reconstruction, and the long middle where you're not sure which one you're doing. All three belong here.

Why me for this

Religious studies at CU Boulder, before the therapy degree.

I studied religion before I studied therapy. That means I take it seriously as something people actually lived inside, with its own logic and its own comforts, rather than as a set of bad opinions to be argued out of someone.

It also means I'm not shocked by the specifics. You can use the real words for what happened, including the parts that sound absurd from outside, without stopping to supply background.

Deconstruction

Grief, anger and relief, usually at once.

Leaving a belief system takes more than deciding it isn't true. The belief came bundled with a community, a calendar, a vocabulary for suffering, and often your family. Losing the claim is the easy part. Losing the structure is what wakes people up at three in the morning.

People are frequently surprised by how much grief is in this, and how much anger sits right next to the relief without canceling it out.

Reconstruction

If you want it.

Some people rebuild. Some find their way to a different tradition, or to the same one held differently. Others want nothing to do with any of it again, and that's a complete answer too.

I'm not steering you. I have my own practice and my own history with this, and neither is the point of your hour.

The long middle

Not knowing yet is a real place.

Leaving a belief system is not the same as leaving yourself.

Most of the people I see are not at either end. They're somewhere in the middle, no longer believing it and not yet anything else, which is lonely in a way that's hard to describe to people who haven't been there.

You can sit in that middle for a while here. It isn't a stage to be moved through on a schedule.
