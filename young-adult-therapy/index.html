Who I work with

Finding your footing.

Therapy for sixteen to twenty four year olds. Video anywhere in Colorado, in person in Boulder when it works out.

This stretch of life

Sixteen to twenty four.

The questions get enormous somewhere around sixteen, and the language for them doesn't show up until years later. That gap is miserable, and most of the adults around you will try to close it by answering questions you didn't ask.

I work with young adults who are taking all of this seriously. You'll get talked to like someone capable of depth, because you are, and because being handled gently by one more adult is probably not what you came for.

If you're the young person

What actually happens.

You bring what's going on. I ask about it. Sometimes I ask what your body is doing while you talk, which sounds odd the first time and stops being odd quickly.

Identity usually comes up, whether that's gender, sexuality, faith, what you want to do with your life, or who you are when your family isn't in the room. None of it's a phase I'm waiting out. It's a shape still forming, and I'd like to be useful while it does.

You can tell me I'm wrong. Please do. I'd rather be corrected than agreed with politely.

If you're the parent

Where the lines are.

My client is your kid, which shapes how this goes.

I'll talk with you about logistics: scheduling, payment, safety concerns. I won't report back on what your kid said in session. That confidentiality is the reason therapy works for this age group at all, and the moment it's conditional, it stops being useful.

If something comes up that genuinely requires your involvement, I'll tell your kid first, and then we'll figure out together how you get told. That's the arrangement I'd want if it were my family.
