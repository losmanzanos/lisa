The ones people ask on the call.

Twelve questions that come up almost every time, answered here so you don't have to spend your free twenty minutes on them.

Questions

If yours isn't here, the free call is a good place to put it.

  • What is contemplative psychotherapy?

    A model developed at Naropa University that starts from the premise that you're already basically sane, and that the difficult parts of you are responses to something rather than defects in you. In practice it means less time diagnosing you and more time getting curious about what you're already doing and why it made sense.

  • What happens in a first session?

    We talk about what brought you. I'll ask some history, but it isn't an interrogation and the paperwork is short. Most people leave the first session knowing whether they want a second one, which is the main thing I'm hoping for.

  • How long will I be in therapy?

    As long as it's useful and not one session longer. Some people come for a few months around a specific thing. Others stay for years. I'll tell you when I think you're done, and you're allowed to disagree.

  • What does it cost?

    $175 for a fifty five minute session. The first twenty minute consult is free. Full rates.

  • Do you take insurance?

    I accept CO Access Medicaid. If you have out of network benefits through a different plan, ask on the consult call and I'll tell you what documentation I can provide.

  • Do you meet in person or by video?

    Mostly video, anywhere in Colorado. I'm in Boulder, and in person is sometimes possible, so ask if that matters to you.

  • Do you work with couples?

    No. Individuals only. If you want couples work I can usually suggest someone.

  • How old do your clients have to be?

    Sixteen and up. A lot of my work is with the sixteen to twenty four range.

  • What is IFS, in one paragraph?

    Internal Family Systems treats you as a system of parts rather than one voice, and assumes even the harsh ones are protecting you. Instead of arguing with the part of you that won't stop rehearsing conversations, we find out what it's worried would happen if it stopped. More on how I work.

  • Will we do EMDR?

    Maybe, and not right away. EMDR is for memories that still arrive at full volume with your body reacting as though it's happening now. When that's what's going on, it can move things that talking has only circled. When it isn't, we won't use it.

  • Do you prescribe medication?

    No. I'm a licensed professional counselor, not a prescriber. I'll coordinate with whoever handles that for you.

  • What if we're not a good fit?

    Then I'll say so, and I'll help you find someone better suited. That's a normal outcome, not a failure, and it's much cheaper to discover in a free consult than in month three.
