Meditation did not fix me

Lisa Wallace, LPC · October 14, 2026

I started meditating because I'd run out of other ideas. That's less inspiring than the version where someone discovers stillness on a retreat, but it's what happened.

I was in pain and I was depressed, and the things that were supposed to work weren't. So I sat down. Then I did it again the next day, and for a long time after that, with Micheal Gregory at Mindfulness Meditation Centers.

Here is what it did not do: it did not make me calm, it did not resolve the pain, and it did not deliver a single insight that would look good on a poster.

Here's what it did do. It gave me enough distance from my own mind to notice that I was queer. Not decide. Notice. The information had been sitting there for years, drowned out by the volume of everything else, and sitting still turned the volume down enough for me to hear it.

It also made me aware of my privilege as a White person in a way that reading hadn't managed, because it's harder to argue with something you're watching yourself do in real time than with something on a page.

When I use mindfulness with clients, this is what I'm after. Not relaxation. Just enough quiet that the information you already have can finally reach you.
